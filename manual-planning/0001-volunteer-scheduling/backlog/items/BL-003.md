**Status**: Parked
**Feature area**: Schedule Builder — cycle rail (originally Spec F1 / `specs/013-schedule-builder`, resurfaced post-023)

## Summary

A ministry leader should be able to mark a specific volunteer as excluded from the scheduling rail — hiding them from the volunteer pool and from recommendations — without removing them from the ministry.

## Full Context

### Why the original framing is dead

This item was written against the per-event Schedule Builder, where the volunteer pool was a sidebar scoped to one event. **That surface no longer exists.** Verified against `develop` @ `b156d65`:

- The pool is now the **cycle-wide rail** — `use-volunteer-pool.ts:57-91` and `volunteer-pool-sidebar.tsx` — with name and role filters plus focus ranking.
- Eligibility resolves per **shift × role**, not per event.
- No exclusion concept exists anywhere: grepping `apps/server/src` and `apps/web/src` for `exclu` returns only the unrelated `excludeSlotId`, `excludeAssignmentId` and `EXCLUDED_PLAN`.

So "hidden for this event only" no longer describes anything the UI does. The 017 reshape's mapping of the old event concept onto `MinistryParticipation` still holds and is carried forward — but the *pool surface* the leader actually looks at is cycle-wide, which is what breaks the original scoping.

### The motivating case, unchanged

There are legitimate cases where a volunteer has not submitted unavailability — they are abroad, or they told the leader verbally — and the leader wants them out of view to reduce noise and avoid accidentally assigning them. The normal path remains the volunteer submitting unavailability through the standard availability flow, after which conflict detection surfaces them.

### ⚠️ Open design question — the scoping unit

**Deliberately not decided here.** Exclusion could key on any of:

- `(volunteer, PlanningCycle)` — out of view for the whole cycle;
- `(volunteer, MinistryParticipation)` — out of view for one ministry's participation in the cycle;
- `(volunteer, Shift)` — out of view for a specific shift only.

These behave very differently on a cycle-wide rail, and deciding between them is a design pass on an unqueued item. Whoever promotes this item decides it then.

### What exclusion must mean, whatever the scoping unit

- The excluded volunteer is hidden from the rail and dropped from recommendations within the chosen scope.
- Any assignments that already exist are **flagged for review, not silently deleted**.
- The exclusion is **reversible** by the leader.
- It does **not** touch ministry membership, availability records, or visibility outside the chosen scope.

### What exclusion is NOT

- A substitute for the volunteer submitting proper availability.
- A permanent or ministry-level action.
- A way to remove someone from the ministry — that belongs in Ministry settings.

## Prerequisites for Implementation

1. A decision on the scoping unit (see above) before any schema work.
2. An exclusion record in the data model, keyed on whatever that decision produces.
3. A rail-level action to exclude and un-exclude, plus a place excluded volunteers remain visible enough to be reversed — the original "collapsed Excluded section at the bottom of the sidebar" idea still applies to the rail.
4. Existing assignments from an excluded volunteer must surface a warning rather than disappearing.
5. RBAC: only leaders (and sub-leaders for their team) can exclude volunteers.
